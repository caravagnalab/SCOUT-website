SCOUT at a glance

A large-scale synthetic benchmarking resource for reproducible cancer genomics analyses, tumour evolution studies, and workflow validation.

Matched tumour–normal whole-genome sequencing (WGS) samples spanning diverse biological and technical conditions, including multiple tumour types, multiregion and longitudinal sampling, and systematically varied tumour purity and sequencing depth.

Comprehensive characterization of seven tumour types, including five solid cancers and two haematological malignancies.

Synthetic paired-end FASTQ datasets generated to emulate Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencing, including 150 bp reads, realistic base-calling quality profiles, and empirically calibrated sequencing error models that reflect modern short-read sequencing platforms.

Systematic benchmarking of somatic and germline variant callers, copy number alteration methods, tumour purity estimators, and evolutionary inference frameworks for subclonal reconstruction and phylogenetic modelling.

Purity/Coverage Conditions
Controlled simulation across a structured grid of tumour purity levels and sequencing depths, including high-depth whole-genome sequencing (up to 150x) and progressively lower-purity and lower-coverage conditions reflecting realistic clinical and research scenarios.

Core features of SCOUT

SCOUT integrates curated genomic references and cancer-specific knowledge to generate biologically realistic tumour genomes and evolutionary dynamics across multiple scales.

Germline genetic foundation
Simulations are grounded in real human genetic diversity using germline haplotypes derived from the 1000 Genomes Project, ensuring realistic population-level background variation in all tumour–normal samples.
Cancer Gene Census–driven drivers
Tumour-specific driver alterations are informed by the Cancer Gene Census, enabling realistic oncogenic architectures that reflect known cancer pathways and disease-specific mutational landscapes.
COSMIC mutational processes
Mutational processes are modelled using COSMIC signatures, capturing context-dependent substitution patterns and indel formation mechanisms that reproduce tumour-type-specific mutational spectra.
Empirical copy-number landscapes
Copy-number alterations are derived from PCAWG and Hartwig datasets, reproducing realistic patterns of aneuploidy, focal amplifications, and deletions across diverse cancer types.
Multi-scale evolutionary modelling
SCOUT integrates clonal expansion, spatial growth, and treatment-driven selection into a unified framework, enabling coherent evolution across cellular, genomic, and population scales.

Why SCOUT matters

SCOUT is designed to reflect the structured, multi-layered complexity of cancer evolution, enabling benchmarking under realistic biological, technical, and evolutionary constraints rather than isolated perturbations.

Cancer genomes are interconnected systems
Genomic signals such as variant allele frequencies, copy-number states, mutational signatures, and phylogenetic structure arise jointly from shared evolutionary processes, producing correlated patterns across multiple molecular layers.
Beyond synthetic spike-ins
SCOUT replaces simplified perturbation benchmarks with explicit evolutionary ground truth, capturing tumour growth, subclonal diversification, and treatment-driven selection in a unified simulation framework.
Clinical realism in WGS analysis
The framework reproduces realistic whole-genome sequencing conditions, including variable purity, sequencing depth, and tumour heterogeneity, supporting robust evaluation of cancer genomics methods in clinically relevant settings.
Mechanistic evaluation of inference
Methods are assessed against explicit evolutionary mechanisms, including clonal ancestry, spatial structure, mutational processes, and treatment selection, enabling direct comparison between inferred outputs and true tumour histories.

What’s next?

Ongoing developments expanding SCOUT toward single-cell resolution, multi-omic integration, and richer models of tumour evolution and phenotype.

Single-cell DNA sequencing benchmarking
Extension of benchmarking to single-cell DNA sequencing (scDNA-seq) technologies, including DLP+-like and scWGS protocols. This will enable evaluation of SNV and copy-number calling performance, as well as phylogenetic reconstruction accuracy at single-cell resolution under realistic noise and coverage constraints.
Multi-omic tumour evolution modelling
Integration of phenotypic evolution with genomic changes through coupled simulation of epigenetic regulation and transcriptional programs, enabling joint modelling of genotype–phenotype interplay during tumour progression.
Single-cell transcriptomics and chromatin accessibility
New simulated experimental layers including single-cell RNA-seq and single-cell ATAC-seq, capturing gene expression dynamics and chromatin accessibility landscapes across evolving tumour subclones.

Software & Pipelines

Software tools and analytical workflows used to generate and analyse the SCOUT cohort.

ProCESS
We generated SCOUT using ProCESS (Programmable Cancer Evolution Spatial Simulator), a framework that integrates spatial tumour evolution with synthetic whole-genome sequencing. Within this framework,we constructed seven tumour prototypes in SCOUT, designed to reflect the characteristic evolutionary trajectories and biological heterogeneity of their corresponding cancer types.
nf-core/sarek
We benchmarked somatic and germline variant calling, as well as copy-number detection methods, for tumour–normal whole-genome sequencing using nf-core/sarek. Across controlled combinations of tumour purity, sequencing depth, and genomic profiles, we evaluated the accuracy of SNV and indel detection, copy-number segmentation, and ploidy inference, alongside tumour purity estimation, using ProCESS-derived ground-truth genomic profiles.
nf-core/tumourevo
We benchmarked tumour evolution inference methods using nf-core/tumourevo, focusing on driver mutation detection, subclonal deconvolution in both single-sample and multi-sample settings, and mutational signature analysis. Across ProCESS-generated ground-truth evolutionary scenarios, we evaluated the accuracy of reconstructed clonal architectures and mutational processes, providing a comprehensive assessment of downstream genomic interpretation of tumour evolution.

Reproducibility

Resources and containers for fully reproducible analyses.

SCOUT Package
An R package providing streamlined access to SCOUT datasets generated via ProCESS. It enables efficient download and querying of processed sequencing data, along with convenient functions to access associated evolutionary ground truth and benchmarking results. The package supports reproducible downstream analyses and integration into cancer genomics workflows.
Analysis code
A public GitHub repository containing all scripts required to reproduce the analyses presented in the SCOUT study, including data processing workflows, benchmarking procedures, and figure generation.
ProCESS Container
A containerised computing environment providing full reproducibility of the SCOUT analysis pipeline. The Docker image includes all dependencies, tools, and workflow configurations required to regenerate the analyses and figures, ensuring consistent execution across computational environments.
